In my memory there are always things I cannot forget. They are like songs — some surging, some gentle, some warm. Through more than a year of junior high, there has been one song that always plays when I feel sad or upset.

It happened on an ordinary Monday, yet something quite extraordinary took place. When I got home at noon and took off my uniform jacket, I found that someone had written a big insulting word on my back. I was so angry I stamped my feet. Though I wanted to find the culprit and have it out with him right away, making those two characters disappear was the urgent thing. I furiously soaked the jacket in water and scrubbed it — but to my even greater fury, even disinfectant couldn't defeat those stubborn characters.

In the afternoon I went to school and, the moment I walked in, grabbed the boy behind me, Sun, and demanded whether he had written it. He panicked at once and stammered that it wasn't him. His obviously guilty manner made me suspect him all the more.

Convinced I had found the culprit, I dragged Sun to our teacher, Sister Yuan. I made my angry accusation, and Sun began to defend himself anxiously in turn. After hearing us out, she did not immediately say who was right and who was wrong. Instead she called over several other classmates and questioned them one by one. Then she said to me in a slow, gentle voice: "Are you really sure he wrote it on your back? The others either say they didn't see him do it, or that they saw the writing on your back as early as this morning. I understand that you've been insulted by words that won't wash off, and that it's unfair. But to blame the wrong person because of that — isn't that going too far? In any case, since we can't find who wrote it, let's take a step back and make room for the sea and sky."

Listening, I shed tears of regret. Yes — how could I vent my anger on someone else without enough evidence? Wasn't that deeply unfair?

That is why Sister Yuan moved me. The song full of her care for her students and her patient guidance still rings in my heart.
