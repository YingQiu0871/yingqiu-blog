## A fund is a rule

In May 2021 the momentum ETF MTUM replaced an estimated 68% of its portfolio in one scheduled rebalance: financials went from 1.5% to a third, technology from 42% to 18%. The ticker stayed the same. A factor fund is a selection rule, and the holder does not know what it will pick next.

## Why factors pay, and fade

Fama and French showed in 1992 that size and book-to-market explained average returns better than market beta. Three explanations compete: compensation for risk, other investors' mistakes, and data mining. McLean and Pontiff found that 97 published predictors earned 26% less out of sample and 58% less after publication.

## Long droughts

Value lagged from 2007 to 2020; the first quarter of 2020 was the standard value factor's worst since 1963. AQR's Cliff Asness argued it was not dead, pointing to a valuation spread near 12 times against a median of 5.4. Momentum crashes in rebounds: from March to May 2009 past losers rose 163% and past winners 8%. The low-volatility ETF USMV fell 33.1% in early 2020, almost as much as the market, and over the past five years has returned less than half as much as SPY.

## Key points

- A factor is a shared trait that helps explain returns.
- Published factors usually earn less afterwards.
- "Works in the long run" can mean thirteen bad years.
- Momentum is most dangerous in sharp recoveries after a crash.
- A factor ETF is a tilt, with no promise of beating the index.
