## What is being measured

These gauges measure the crowd, not companies. VIX and put/call ratios come from real option trades and show what people pay for protection. The AAII survey records what individuals say. FINRA margin debt shows how much they borrow. Breadth counts how many stocks take part in a move. CNN's Fear and Greed Index averages seven such inputs.

## Panic has a floor, greed has none

The best record is at bottoms. AAII bearishness hit a record 70.3% on 5 March 2009, days before the low. VIX closed at a record 82.69 on 16 March 2020; the S&P 500 bottomed a week later, so even a correct signal came with further losses first. At tops the signals arrive far too early. In 2021 the equity put/call ratio sat near 0.4 for many months, margin debt peaked around $936 billion in October, and breadth thinned while the Nasdaq 100 set a record on 19 November. All were right eventually, after flashing for a year.

## When the gauge becomes the bet

On 5 February 2018 the S&P 500 fell 4.2% and VIX rose 20 points. Inverse and leveraged volatility products had to buy VIX futures at the close, feeding the move. XIV lost more than 80% in a day and was wound up.

## Key points

- Sources differ: trades, surveys and borrowing are not equally reliable.
- Extreme fear is a better signal than extreme optimism.
- A correct contrarian signal can still be early by days at bottoms and by a year at tops.
- Narrow breadth shows an index leaning on a few stocks.
- Products built on VIX can distort VIX itself.
