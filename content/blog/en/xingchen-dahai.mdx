> Class 1, Grade 12 · Gu Yuning · Teacher: Wan Lifang

The cicadas outside the window kept chirping. The two white geese kept by the school's gatekeeper were frolicking in the shade, chasing each other with flapping wings; the hill behind the school basked lazily in the sun. I pulled my mind back to the paper and saw the gaokao essay topic — a thousand thoughts surged in my heart, and a thousand words, yet I did not know where to begin.

In the last review class of senior year, the teacher said: "Study hard. In a few days, when the exams are over, we'll have a good reunion at the celebration banquet — it won't be easy to meet again after that." Only then did I truly, sharply realise that my three years of high school were about to end. Twelve years of hard study, seizing every moment. I couldn't help thinking: after these twelve years of life, how should I plan the next chapter?

First, I think, I must be an honest person, true to myself.

As the saying goes, "writing reveals the person." In the coming university life, there will surely be much independent research, which demands higher standards of myself. The beginning of scholarship is the art of being a person. So I will insist on honesty and self-accountability in the next stage of study and research — no plagiarism, no cheating. Earlier this year an actor was exposed for academic fraud: a plagiarised thesis, a degree obtained through connections, all to market a "straight-A student" persona. I must take that as a warning.

Second, I should be ambitious, with goals, and the spirit to strive for them.

President Xi Jinping has said: "Young people should aim high." And the old saying goes: "Where there is a will, there is a way." I should plan each period of my time and strive with direction and purpose. Rather than drifting through university — mediocre, muddled, wasting my days — better to throw myself into the work and strive toward my goals in life. See Tu Youyou, who from childhood set her heart on healing, toiled for years, and at last, with effort never betraying her, produced artemisinin to conquer malaria, contributing to the cause of all humanity. See Dr Sun Yat-sen, who from the day he saw his sister's feet bound stood firm on his great purpose of rescuing four hundred million Chinese from fire and water: he studied hard, travelled east and west, endured hardship, studied Western political systems, and at last founded the Republic, ending the Qing dynasty's more than 260 years of cruel rule.

Most important of all: my personal ideals should be bound up with the fate of my country.

Man is a social animal; no one can ever exist wholly apart from the collective and society. A man who lives entirely for himself is utterly selfish. When the nest is overturned, no egg remains whole: cut off from the nation, we can barely take a step. There was Qian Xuesen, who braved countless dangers to return to China and develop missiles and rockets to guard the nation's peace; and Yuan Longping, who buried himself in the fields, devoting himself to research so that more than a billion Chinese could eat their fill. My ideals, too, should not be divorced from reality; they should proceed from facts, seek truth from facts. Useful to society, beneficial to the collective — only so can the small self be fulfilled and the greater self achieved.

On the road of life there are always turning points, signposts. Now I stand before the crossroads of the gaokao, checking my luggage, composing my mind, preparing for the next stage of life. I know well that the scenery behind, though beautiful, can only be savoured; the road ahead, though uncertain, is my own journey. Rather than hesitating, let me carve out a world for myself!

Today, at last, I sat in this chair that had waited so long for me, in this examination hall rehearsed countless times. What met my eyes was the sight of classmates writing furiously, shirts stuck to skin with sweat. This is the first battle of my new journey. Now — let me fire the first shot!

I raised my head and looked out the window; the glaring sunlight dazzled my eyes. The hot summer wind rushed over my face, and the nagging cicadas sang loud. My eyes quickly adjusted to the glare. Like a soldier drawing his sword, I strode out toward the next examination hall of life.

My journey is the sea of stars!
