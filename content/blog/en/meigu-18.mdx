## What they measure

Every indicator is computed from past prices and past volume. Volume and support or resistance ask how many people committed at a price. RSI, MACD and Bollinger Bands are arithmetic on price itself: how fast it has moved and how far it sits from its own average. The three are close relatives, which matters later.

## One reading, two meanings

"Overbought" works tolerably in a range and fails in a trend. In January 2018 the S&P 500's 14-month RSI passed 87 for the first time in almost 22 years, according to Oppenheimer's Ari Wald. The last such reading, in June 1996, was followed by a further 128% gain. This time the index fell 10% in nine trading days. In 2022 the mirror image appeared: after sitting 17.1% below its 200-day average in June, the index rallied to within 0.2% of that line in August and turned down again.

## Lag and stacking

Indicators lag because they are averages. Shorter settings react sooner and produce more false signals. Stacking three indicators built from the same closing prices repeats one fact three times. Tuning parameters on history is data snooping. Bajgrowicz and Scaillet (2012), using the Dow from 1897 to 2011, found that low transaction costs offset in-sample performance and that the best rules could not be chosen in advance.

## Key points

- Indicators describe what has happened; none contains tomorrow's price.
- Decide first whether the market is trending or ranging, then read the number.
- Faster settings trade lag for noise.
- Combining related indicators adds no independent evidence.
- Backtested parameter choices rarely survive costs and out-of-sample tests.
