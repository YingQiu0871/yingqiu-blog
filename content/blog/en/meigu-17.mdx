## Why trends exist

Textbooks say news should be priced at once. The data disagree. Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (2012) looked at 58 futures and forward contracts and found that a positive past twelve-month return predicted further gains in every one of them. Their explanation is behavioural: investors under-react at first, then over-react, and part of the move reverses later. Forced selling by leveraged holders adds to it.

## What a moving average buys

Meb Faber's rule is one line: hold the S&P 500 when the month-end price is above its ten-month average, otherwise hold cash. Summaries of his 2013 update report roughly 10.2% a year against 9.3% for buy-and-hold from 1901 to 2012, with volatility of 12.0% against 17.9%. I would not trust the extra return; the parameter was chosen with hindsight and costs are ignored. The smaller drawdown is the real result. The SG Trend Index, which tracks large trend-following funds, gained 20.9% in 2008 and 27.4% in 2022.

## When it fails

Trend rules lose in choppy markets and in sharp reversals. One StockCharts test of the S&P 500 and its 200-day average from 1994 to 2019 found 90 buy signals, of which only 20 made money. The 2010s offered few large moves to follow, and the SG Trend Index fell 20.4% between May 2024 and May 2025.

## Key points

- Trend is the best-documented pattern in technical analysis, across stocks, bonds, commodities and currencies.
- Moving-average rules mainly reduce losses in long declines; they do not reliably add return.
- Most signals are false, so the method depends on tolerating many small losses.
- Range-bound markets and V-shaped reversals are where it breaks.
