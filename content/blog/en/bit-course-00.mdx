> Source: the public speech transcript of the Bit Langlang live course compiled by io.traderax.net · 32 sessions · 9 September 2023 to 1 June 2024

## The material

Bit Langlang is a crypto futures trader who ran a series of public live sessions on the Bcoin forum between late 2023 and mid 2024. io.traderax.net ran speech recognition over 32 recordings and published the result as a single transcript of roughly 320,000 Chinese characters, with the teacher's spoken style, audience interaction and repetition left in.

This column is a second edit of that transcript. Each lesson is one article; lesson number, date and original title are kept. The source video filename and duration sit in the quote block at the top of every article.

Two pairs of files in the transcript are two recognition runs of the same stream: lessons 15 and 18 (the 13 January 2024 session; lesson 15's stated date of 1 January does not match its content) and lessons 21 and 22 (2 March 2024). One article is kept for each pair, so lessons 15 and 22 have no article of their own and the column holds 30 lessons under their original numbers.

## What the edit did

- Removed chat with the audience, small talk, community promotion and replies to individual viewers.
- Kept each point once and dropped filler words.
- Reorganised each session around its methods rather than the video timeline; trades discussed are compressed into short cases: setup, reason, entry, stop, outcome, lesson.
- Fixed recognition errors (coin names, technical terms).
- Kept every substantive point about method: how stages are identified, the entry logic and stop placement of each trade discussed, calls on specific coins, the numbers given, and the rules about position size and discipline.
- Added no commentary and no disclaimers.

"Now", "today" and "next week" in the text refer to the time of recording. The markets and coins discussed belong to 2023 and 2024.

## The arc of the course

There was no syllabus; each session followed that week's market. Roughly four phases:

- Lessons 01 to 07, September to November 2023: the foundation. Sentiment cycles, how to tell the early, middle and late phase of a range, why there is no certainty inside a range, breakout entry conditions, and the three elements of a good trade.
- Lessons 08 to 14, December 2023: late-stage problems. Catch-up rallies and exit signals, why stops fail to get executed, what leaders have in common during rotation, the difference between being bullish and going long.
- Lessons 16 to 26, January to March 2024: homework for the correction. Correction scale and market cycle, patience in a range, spotting counter-trend trades from the market environment, resonance and catch-up opportunities, and recovering discipline after a loss.
- Lessons 27 to 32, April to June 2024: position and execution once the market moves again. Stage-based position sizing, breakouts and stops inside a range, entries and exits in a main uptrend, counter-trend shorts with scaled take-profit, leader identification and the second wave.

## Rules that run through every lesson

1. Trading logic rests on dividing the market into stages: launch at the end of a range, main uptrend, high-level range, main downtrend, range again. Decide the scale and cycle first, then look for an entry.
2. A good trade has three conditions: an entry logic, a small and explicit stop, and a calm holding process. Targets and stops are set by the entry logic, not by intraday moves.
3. There is no certainty inside a range. Certainty appears only where the structure is complete and price has converged to the point of choosing a direction.
4. In a main uptrend, long only. Hold trend trades without scaling out; take half off counter-trend trades early.
5. Read cycle and room on the large timeframe, find the entry on the small one, execute on the one-minute chart.
6. Until you are consistently profitable, keep only a few hundred to a couple of thousand in the account. Capital is the least important thing in this game.
7. Review is the only way to learn: replay historical charts, study your own trades, study other people's trades. Books do not help.

## How to read

Reading in lesson order is easiest, since later sessions assume the earlier vocabulary. For the skeleton of the method, start with lessons 01 and 02 (cycle stages and ranges), 19 (reading counter-trend trades from the market environment) and 27 (the three-dimension system); the other sessions apply the same method to that week's trades. Each article ends with a key-points list.
