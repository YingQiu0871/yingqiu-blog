> Source: public posts by @WallStreet0Name from 2025 and 2026, taken from the first two sections of the [Trader-Archives collection](https://suoha888.github.io/Trader-Archives/wallstreet0name/), about 120 curated posts. This is my own summary, not a reprint.

He entered the market in 2018 and trades mainly bitcoin and gold. His method is scattered across posts, with the same idea returning under new examples. I merged the repeats and ordered them the way a chart is read. The archive's thirteen long articles are equity research and AI prompts and are not used here.

## Every tool is learnable; none is final

He has studied the major schools and finds them largely the same. In ordinary moves the technically literate ride with large money. In abnormal moves the people who lose are the technicians sure that price has "reached the level". Price is disorderly about four fifths of the time, so the job is to wait for money to start moving, predict if you like, and concede at once when price disagrees.

## Reading order

Weekly, daily, four-hour, one-hour, on a clean chart. Bare candles first, then a trend average, then a VWAP anchored at a key starting point, then a fixed-range volume profile for ranges. He uses divergence the opposite way to the textbooks: in a strong advance he buys the pullback and rides the leg that will later print the divergence. His named mistake is trading with the small timeframe against the large one.

## Treat a consolidation as one object

Ask four questions: what follows a break up, a break down, and the failure of each.

- A real breakout of a daily high should leave quickly; lingering usually means a false one.
- He rarely anticipates. He waits for a confirmed break and enters on the retest, with the stop back inside the range.
- Resistance is where a long is reduced, never a reason to open a short.
- Before chasing, ask whether you are following the trend or supplying exit liquidity.

## Cost levels

VWAP and the volume profile answer one question: where is the average holder's cost? A rebound to the VWAP of the prior sell-off is a take-profit zone. After leaving a range, the next dense area is the target. If price returns inside the range, the breakout view was wrong and he exits.

## Sizing, stops, withdrawals

- Decide the maximum loss before entering.
- Size so that you can sleep with a stop in place.
- Go heavy only at trend starts and turning points; once price leaves cost, close enough to cover the stop.
- Keep a small slice of capital in the derivatives account and refill only after it is gone.
- Withdraw profits. He stopped pyramiding years ago.
- Cancel resting orders before sleep.

Bear markets hurt through long disorderly ranges: bottom-fishing money is ground away before the bottom arrives.

## Key points

- Location across timeframes before any indicator.
- A breakout that does not leave fast is treated as false.
- Enter on the retest with the stop inside the range.
- Stay out during chop so capital survives to the real move.
- He advises ordinary people to avoid leveraged contracts altogether.
