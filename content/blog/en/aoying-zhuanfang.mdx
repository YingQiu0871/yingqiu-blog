> Source: three interviews with Aoying (@thankUcrypto): OKX "Talking to Traders" (24 June 2025), Binance Chinese Crypto Radio (18 July 2025) and the OKX follow-up (13 October 2025), about 40,000 Chinese characters in total. Full text in the [Trader-Archives collection](https://suoha888.github.io/Trader-Archives/thankucrypto/). This is my own summary, not a reprint.

Aoying was an internet product manager who began trading futures in 2021 and later topped the copy-trading boards of two exchanges. His self-reported figures differ between interviews, so these notes leave the numbers aside and keep the practices he repeats. He says he has no system. Reading all three, there is a fixed order of judgement underneath.

## The small-account ceiling is psychological

He could turn a few thousand into six figures and then give it back, again and again, because he would pull his stop when price turned against him. Study did not fix that; being close to wiped out did. His rule for small accounts is a hard stop on every trade. He often trades without one now, and explains why: most of his profit has been withdrawn.

## Regime before method

Before any order he asks whether the market is ranging or trending. In a range he sells highs and buys lows; in a trend he holds. He once fell to minus forty percent in a competition by betting on breakouts inside a range, then recovered by switching approach. His image is motor racing: flat out on the straight, slow in the corners. A bear market is all corners, so he mostly sits out. His motto about speed means acting decisively in the few days he reads best, and has nothing to do with high leverage.

## Indicators as reference, logic as the reason

He has studied the common systems and keeps only candles, moving averages and volume, since an indicator's hit rate depends on the regime. His entries come from events and sentiment: a lead from equities, a liquidation scare, money that missed a rally looking for laggards, shorts being squeezed. He warns that news is sometimes attached to a move after the fact, and that an event type stops working once the market has learned it.

## Leverage, compounding, stops

- Screens show 10x; after withdrawals and staged entries his effective leverage is 4x to 5x on majors and below 1x on small caps. He advises staying under 5x.
- He does not pyramid. He closes each trade and sizes the next from the larger balance.
- Stops follow the reason for entry: a breakout trade is closed if price returns to cost.
- For short-term work, payoff ratio matters more than win rate.
- If trading feels painful, size or leverage is too large.

## Copy trading and survivorship

He credits his ranking to letting followers exit first. Later he saw his public calls being used as exit liquidity and stopped posting positions. After a large drawdown on a public short he closed copy trading altogether, concluding that someone always joins at the peak. He insists his path cannot be repeated: the people who lost everything have left and are not heard. His advice to newcomers is no debt, no money that affects daily life, and leaving if it does not suit you.

## Key points

- Identify range or trend before choosing a method.
- Press hard only in the stretch you read best.
- Keep effective leverage low enough to hold and to cut.
- Let the entry logic decide the stop.
- Success stories omit those who quit after losing.
