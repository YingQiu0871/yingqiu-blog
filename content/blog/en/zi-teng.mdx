Faced with hard setbacks, I used to be like most people my age: angry and desperate, or simply ready to give up. But when I saw that wisteria in glorious bloom, I changed my mind completely.

This happened when I was in primary school. In sixth grade, our teacher set an incredibly difficult IT assignment, worth a huge 20 points: using XPLogo to write commands that make the little turtle draw a 9×9 two-colour chessboard with 36 commands. As a boy who had always loved IT, I was full of confidence.

I spent three classes painstakingly producing a multi-coloured chessboard — using just 16 lines of code. Yet this perfect work met the worst possible ending: during the transfer, thanks to outdated software, a broken network and I don't know what else, my assignment shamelessly vanished. Just like that. What was I supposed to make of it? I had worked so hard for so long, spending so much precious time and energy, and now there was nothing left at all. In a fit of temper I simply stopped, turned around and walked out of the classroom — it was only 20 points after all!

When I stepped out of the house the next morning, my mood still hadn't recovered. Why was my luck so bad? How could such a freak disaster strike me? I thought angrily. But as I passed the garden, I saw clusters of purple wisteria blooming wantonly on the old wooden arbour, facing the rising sun. I had never imagined that the frail-looking wisteria, after this winter's cold snap, could still hold such vigorous life. In contrast, the evergreen trees nearby had all had their frozen-dead branches "beheaded".

Before my eyes appeared a picture: on cold nights, the wind howling, a few fragile wisteria vines drifting helplessly in the cold. Day after day, their once-green leaves and branches were reduced to withered bare stems. Spring came, and in the gentle breeze, tiny green buds pushed out of the dead wood. A month later, at last, little violet flowers opened.

Ah — wasn't this the simple truth being told to me: persistence is victory? If I let such a small setback make me lose heart, that really wouldn't be me.

In the following two days I finished the assignment in the computer lab — and it was even better than before.

Thank you, lush wisteria, for making me truly understand what persistence and never giving up mean. Yes — this old, familiar lesson, I finally understood that day.
