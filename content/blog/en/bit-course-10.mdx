## Three signs you can make it

Trading is a visible path to a different life, and it is extremely hard. If you do not believe you can do it, you have no chance. Entries and stops cannot be explained in a sentence; every trade has its own logic.

Three things tell you an edge is forming. Given a chart, you can read the cycle and the likely path. When a wave comes and it is time to make money, you make it. When the market ranges, you lose little or nothing. Do that across two cycles with a stable mindset and stable profit follows. A 10,000x account can be gone in days; even 10% over two cycles is enough.

## Where the stop goes

A stop is not a fixed distance. It sits at a key level such that if price touches it, the short-term direction is no longer valid. Long at a box bottom, defend the box bottom.

Understanding a move does not make it tradeable. A rise from 38,000 to 40,000 can be a clean push or a grinding range, with a 90% or a 10% chance of profit. I do not trade inside boxes.

## An ORDI long

On the higher timeframe ORDI launched, pushed up and started ranging, so longs were with the trend, taken once the correction was done. The entry came from the 5-minute chart when price returned to the range and a retest held. The first attempt, stop at 52, was stopped out. The second retest made no new low; I went long with a stop at 53.7 and it ran. I took profit early because the setup was not a box bottom and BTC had not moved that day. I trade levels with certainty: eight of ten work for me where eight of ten stop out for you.

## Key points

- Without belief that you can make it, there is no chance; the skill takes years.
- A stable edge means reading the cycle, earning when it is time and losing little when it is not.
- Place the stop at the level that invalidates the short-term direction.
- Readable is not the same as tradeable; stay out of boxes.
- Lower expectations when a coin moves without BTC, and take profit gradually.
- Withdraw regularly, keep only trading capital on the exchange, and split positions.
