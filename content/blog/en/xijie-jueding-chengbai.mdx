Not long ago a saying spread through our class: "Don't mind the details." But can details really be ignored? I don't think so.

By "details" I mean the small links and moments of things — in plain words, the little corners and ends. Traditional thinking holds that details don't matter; there is even the saying "he who achieves great things ignores small matters." In cooking, "a little salt, a little MSG" is the most typical of Eastern habits and traits.

Take the quadratic equations we were studying. In the first few days, my exercise book was full of what I considered trivial problems. Yes — so I didn't simplify; so I left out a "∵" symbol. What's the big deal? But Teacher Cui's merciless red crosses told me it was not so simple: details must be attended to.

Maupassant became a literary giant in no small part because he paid great attention to seeing the truth in details. When he was still learning to write under his teacher Flaubert, Flaubert made him observe a statue of an old woman in Paris three times. Maupassant's observation of the old woman grew from "she is an old woman" to "that old woman's nose is the handiwork of the world's worst carpenter, who whittled a piece of wood and stuck it on her face." Thus, under Flaubert's strict demands, Maupassant came to understand the importance of observing details, gradually improved his writing, and became a great writer. Those who achieve great things must be people who mind details.

Details are ordinary, unremarkable. A word, a gesture, a passing thought… details are as insignificant as grains of sand, easily overlooked — but they must not be taken lightly. They can make the glory of a lifetime, or ruin its reputation.

The Space Shuttle Columbia exploded 80 seconds after liftoff, killing all seven astronauts. The investigation showed that the culprit was a piece of fallen foam that struck the heat shield before the left wing. Was the foam big? Very small! But that one small piece of foam destroyed not only a priceless shuttle but seven lives beyond measure. Here, a slip in one tiny detail wrecked a great project, at terrible cost.

In any setting, the importance of details goes without saying. Don't ignore any seemingly insignificant detail. A journey of a thousand li begins with a single step. Only by doing every step well can we reach the shore of success as quickly as possible.
