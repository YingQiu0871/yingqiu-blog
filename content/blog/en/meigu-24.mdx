## The gap

Morningstar's 2026 Mind the Gap study covers the ten years to the end of 2025. US funds returned 9.9% a year; the average dollar invested in them earned 8.7%. The missing 1.2 points, about 12% of the total, went to badly timed buying and selling. The gap was 0.4 points in plain US equity funds and 0.1 in US equity index funds, 1.2 in sector funds, and about 14 in the first year of crypto ETFs.

## Losses hurt more

Kahneman and Tversky's prospect theory says a loss weighs more than an equal gain; their 1992 estimate was 2.25 times. In a brokerage account this becomes the disposition effect. Odean found investors about 1.5 times as likely to sell a winner as a loser, and the losers they kept trailed the winners they sold by 3.5 points over the next year. Overconfidence adds trading: in Barber and Odean's sample of 66,465 households, the most active earned 11.4% a year while the market returned 17.9%.

## Rules, not willpower

Knowing the biases changes little. Allocation funds, which rebalance on their own, show a gap of only 0.7 points because holders have less to tinker with. The chapter lists a few rules decided in advance: written reasons, fixed dates, position caps, fewer looks at the account.

## Key points

- Investors lose more than a point a year to their own timing.
- Selling winners and keeping losers is the usual pattern, and it costs money.
- The more someone trades, the less they keep.
- Flashier, more volatile products show wider gaps.
- Decide the rule before the emotion arrives.
