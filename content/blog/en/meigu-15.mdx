## Numbers versus expectations

On September 13, 2022, August CPI rose 0.1% on the month when economists expected a 0.1% fall. The S&P 500 dropped 4.32%, its worst day since June 2020. Markets trade the gap between a number and the forecast. CPI arrives first and moves prices most, though the Fed's 2% target is defined on PCE. GDP is late and revised; ISM is a survey, rough but early.

## When good news is bad

On February 2, 2018, payrolls beat forecasts and wages grew 2.9%, the fastest since 2009. The Dow fell 666 points because faster wages implied faster rate hikes. The sign flips with the fear of the moment: when the worry is recession, weak data is plainly bad.

## Signals that disappointed

The two-year and ten-year Treasury curve stayed inverted from July 2022 to September 2024, a record of roughly 26 months, and the recession it warned of did not arrive while stocks climbed. Fidelity's cycle map has consumer discretionary leading early and staples and utilities holding up in recessions, yet no sector wins reliably mid-cycle, and nobody knows the phase in real time.

## Key points

- Reactions depend on surprise, not on the level of the number.
- An inverted curve says "sometime", never "when".
- In 2022 a weak euro cut the S&P 500's loss from 18.1% to 12.7% for euro investors.
- In 2025 a weak dollar cut their gain from 17.9% to 3.9%.
