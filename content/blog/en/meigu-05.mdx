## Four holding periods

A position held for ten minutes and one held for ten years share a ticker and nothing else. Within a day a company's earnings do not change, so a day trader's gain is another trader's loss, minus commissions and spreads. Over years, corporate profits do most of the work and everyone can win together.

## What the account data says

Researchers followed every individual who began day trading Brazilian equity futures in 2013 to 2015 and kept at it for 300 sessions: 97% lost money and roughly 1% out-earned the minimum wage. Fifteen years of Taiwan Stock Exchange records gave the same answer, with under 1% of day traders reliably profitable after fees. Virtu, a market maker on the other side, reported one losing day in 1,238. Barber and Odean's 66,465 US households show the milder version: the most active traders earned 11.4% a year while the market returned 17.9%.

## Pick the horizon first

SPY had six down years out of 33 between 1993 and 2025, yet three of them came in a row. Long-term holders need patience, not speed. Trouble starts when tools and horizons are mixed, or when a losing short-term trade is quietly renamed a long-term holding.

## Key points

- Day trading is a negative-sum game after costs, and the data from Brazil and Taiwan is unambiguous.
- Turnover itself is the cost: the busiest households trailed the market by more than six points a year.
- Decide the holding period first; what to read and which instruments to use follow from it.
- Never change games mid-trade to avoid admitting a loss.
