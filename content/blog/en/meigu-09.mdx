A digest of the Chinese chapter. The first question about any company is which kind of stock it is.

## Growth, value and cyclicals

Nvidia's revenue went from $26.97 billion in fiscal 2023 to $215.9 billion in fiscal 2026. Growth stocks fear one thing: slowing growth. Nvidia lost more than 60% of its value between November 2021 and October 2022 without any real disaster. Value stocks look cheap, sometimes for good reason; Exxon Mobil was dropped from the Dow in August 2020. Exxon is also the cyclical example: a $22.4 billion loss in 2020, a record $55.7 billion profit in 2022. With cyclicals the price-to-earnings ratio looks lowest right at the peak.

## Dividends and turnarounds

Coca-Cola raised its dividend for the 64th straight year in 2026. General Electric shows the other side: its quarterly payout went from 24 cents to 12 cents in 2017 and to one cent in 2018, it left the Dow that June, and it later split into three companies. We remember the turnarounds that worked because the failures were delisted.

## Small caps and Chinese ADRs

About 40% of Russell 2000 companies have negative trailing earnings, according to Apollo. An Alibaba ADR is a receipt for shares in a Cayman holding company that controls part of its business through contracts, and by early 2024 the stock was down about 75% from its 2020 level.

## Key points

- Classify first; the wrong yardstick makes a good company look bad.
- Growth stocks fall hardest when growth merely slows.
- Read a cyclical's P/E upside down.
- A dividend can be cut to a penny.
- Chinese ADRs add structural and regulatory risk on two sides.
