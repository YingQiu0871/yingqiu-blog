## Counter-trend trades and levels of divergence

Most losses this week came from tilt, not analysis. TRB sat in a 15-minute range under 120 after an extended run, which I call a super-large divergence: the monthly chart is at an extreme and the coin needs a long rest. You stay out and only start watching when the range has narrowed for a long time and is about to pick a direction. A second test of the low marks the shift to the late stage of a range. My shorts there had some logic, but they were counter-trend on the higher timeframe, and trend momentum ran straight through my resistance levels.

## Entering after a large-scale breakout

SOL, LINK, AAVE and GST were all entered the same way: a large breakout, consolidation at the high, then a long on a turning point after the top was set, never at a new high. SOL and AAVE worked, LINK and GST stopped out. I sold SOL too early because holding LINK biased me against it. On SOL I also sized three or four times normal, so I would not put the stop at the real invalidation level and was stopped four times while right on direction. Light size is what allows a wide stop at the key level. Selling LINK after a well-formed consolidation failed was defensible; selling BTC with it was not.

## ARK as a textbook leader uptrend

ARK ran a standard leader main uptrend: range breakout, small divergences, a first large one after five waves, then a second leg. I caught the first small divergence with the stop below it, targeting 1. My mistake was dumping the core position on a wild false breakout, then adding at highs and getting stopped five times with nearly 10,000 U of open profit each; holding the core would have avoided all of it. Next week: BTC breakout, SOL second leg (all-in on the breakout), LINK second leg, TRB long while 97 holds.

## Key points

- Counter-trend trades have no good ending; cutting them removes most losses.
- In a super-large divergence, stay out until the range has narrowed at its tail.
- After a large breakout, buy turning points after the top, not new highs.
- Oversizing makes you abandon the correct stop; size light, stop wide.
- Hold a core position with a cost advantage through false breakouts.
- Logic first, then psychology. When tilted, stop, review, write the day's plan.
