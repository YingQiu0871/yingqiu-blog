## Why rates anchor everything

In December 2021 the Fed's dot plot pointed to three small hikes in 2022. Instead it raised rates eleven times by July 2023, to 5.25%-5.50%. The Nasdaq Composite lost 33.1% in 2022 and long Treasury funds such as TLT lost about 31%. Rates work through three channels: they discount future cash flows, they set what safe money earns, and they change what borrowing costs companies and households.

## Meetings, dots and the balance sheet

The FOMC meets eight times a year and publishes projections four times. The dots are individual guesses, not a promise. The balance sheet is the second tool: bond buying in a crisis, then runoff, which ended on December 1, 2025.

## Three episodes

Volcker let the funds rate rise to about 20% and accepted a recession with 10.8% unemployment; stocks bottomed on August 12, 1982, before the jobs data improved. In March 2020 two emergency cuts did not stop the fall, and the S&P 500 bottomed on March 23, the day open-ended bond buying was announced. In 2022 the same force ran in reverse.

## Key points

- Assets with distant cash flows are the most rate-sensitive.
- A cut helps stocks when there is no recession; cuts made during recessions were followed by much weaker returns.
- Markets turn on direction and surprise, ahead of the data.
- "Don't fight the Fed" fails when the Fed itself misjudges, as in 2021.
