A digest of the Chinese chapter, which reads Apple's fiscal 2025 Form 10-K line by line.

## Three questions, three statements

The income statement asks what was left over the year: Apple turned $416.2 billion of sales into $195.2 billion of gross margin, $133.1 billion of operating income and $112.0 billion of net income. The balance sheet is a snapshot of one day: $359.2 billion of assets, $285.5 billion of liabilities, $73.7 billion of equity. Inventory was only $5.7 billion, about nine days of cost of sales. Retained earnings are negative, an "accumulated deficit" of $14.3 billion, because buybacks have hollowed out book equity. The cash flow statement asks whether money really arrived: $111.5 billion from operations, $12.7 billion of capital spending, so $98.8 billion of free cash flow.

## When profit and cash part ways

Profit is recorded under accrual rules; cash is counted. Enron reported $979 million of net income for 2000 using mark-to-market accounting, and $4.78 billion of operating cash flow. The SEC later alleged that "prepay" deals with banks were loans booked as operating cash; a Senate panel counted more than $8 billion of them. Even so, Enron's operating cash flow for the second quarter of 2000 was negative. It filed for bankruptcy on December 2, 2001.

## Key points

- Read the three statements against each other.
- Watch margins layer by layer, and compare them over time.
- Free cash flow is operating cash flow minus capital spending.
- Cash flow can be dressed up by moving items between sections.
- Check the numerator and denominator: Apple's roughly 171% return on equity is a buyback artefact.
