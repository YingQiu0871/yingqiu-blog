> Written in Grade 4 · Gu Yuning (originally published on zuowen.com, 2012-08-06, signed "Niu Bingxun (Nimo)")

In *Finding Nemo*, my favourite character was the little fish called Nimo. I had always dreamed of swimming in the water just like him. During our holiday at the Angsana resort, I finally found that feeling I longed for — in the swimming pool.

One sunny afternoon, Peng Jiajun and I were swimming in the shallow end of the Angsana pool. Plenty of kids were swimming, diving and splashing in the deep end. Watching their nimble bodies, I was full of envy — but I didn't dare get in. Peng Jiajun, though, wasn't scared at all and jumped straight in. The shadows at the bottom of the pool wavered back and forth, as if mocking me for not going in.

The deep end was two metres deep, and that number terrified me: it meant that even my grandpa couldn't touch the bottom, let alone me at 1.48 metres. And if I did touch the bottom, I'd still have 52 centimetres of water above my head. With my swimming skills at the time, floating at all was hard enough — let alone swimming. I stared at the pool in fear, hesitating, not daring to go in.

Under Grandpa's protection, I slowly lowered myself into the deep end. At first I only dared to tread water by the ladder. Then, plucking up my courage, I swam cautiously along the wall, my heart in my mouth the whole time: one moment I forgot to paddle, the next I forgot to kick; one moment I swam too fast and was out of breath, the next too slowly and nearly sank.

After a while I steadied down. My arms and legs began to work together, and my strokes grew relaxed. I found that the water holds you up like an invisible hand — you don't have to move so fast to float, and it's not tiring at all. Before I knew it I had swum nearly 25 metres. Yet only three days earlier, when we had just arrived at the resort, I could barely manage 10.

Soon I was swimming with growing ease, even crossing the pool without Grandpa's help, with no trouble at all. Peng Jiajun and I even had a race. In the first five metres he was still bragging about how good he was — how he'd won gold medals, how the race was "a piece of cake" for him. But around the 20-metre mark he began to slow down, and by the finish line he was limp in the water, floating on a borrowed board while I wasn't tired at all. Watching him gasp for breath, I felt enormously proud — this race, I thought, was really my "piece of cake".

The deep end wasn't scary after all. Even without Grandpa's protection, the deep water opened itself to me. I swam freely in the two-metre-deep pool, thinking: it feels wonderful to be a fish! If you ask which fish — it's the little fish called Nimo.
