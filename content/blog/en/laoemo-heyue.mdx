> Source: Lao Emo, "老恶魔谈合约", 28 March 2025, about 9,500 Chinese characters. Full text in the [Trader-Archives edition](https://suoha888.github.io/Trader-Archives/laoemo/). This is my own summary, not a reprint; the original is authoritative for figures and wording.

The author entered the market in 2017 and trades only BTC and ETH futures, aggressively, at 10x to 20x cross margin. He says up front that this is a review of his own method for experienced, high-risk traders, and that beginners should not copy it.

The whole post answers one question: how does someone who admits to tilting and holding losers avoid going to zero? His answer is not technique. It is a set of gates outside the trading account.

## Capital comes first

- Split deployable capital into 5 to 10 equal parts and fund the account with one part at a time.
- Never add margin to a deeply losing position. It works most of the time, and the one time it fails erases everything.
- Withdraw once a single part has tripled, into fiat if you cannot trust yourself.
- As withdrawn profit accumulates, re-split it into more or larger parts.

His calm, he says, comes from most of his profit no longer being on the exchange.

## Two kinds of trade

He reads candles only and largely ignores news.

- Short-term trades last under a day. Momentum entries beat fading. Target 2% to 6%, stop within 3%, leave at target, and do not reverse right after taking profit.
- Mid-term trades make most of his money. Direction matters more than entry price. A correct one shows profit early. Use 5x to 10x, accept that a single trade may lose most of its stake, and stop watching the balance.

## Stops, targets, pyramiding, leverage

- Set the stop before anything else and never move it. A stop that turns out wrong was still a valid stop.
- Mid-term targets must be far; exit at the price without bargaining.
- Add to a position only on a large open profit in a one-way market, with less than half the original size, and never above the starting leverage.
- All leverage figures are effective leverage. Start at 10x, cut to 5x to 10x when daily range exceeds 10%, and treat 35x as a hard ceiling.
- Very low leverage is not automatically safer: a distant liquidation price invites holding losers.

## Costs and habits

Fees scale with notional size, so he manages them like a cost centre: limit orders for exits, a rebate account, closing manually before liquidation, less time against a high funding rate. Over two years this cut his fees to roughly an eighth of the taker-only figure. He watches charts on a different device and account from the one holding positions.

## Key points

- Capital in parts, one part at a time, no margin top-ups on losers.
- Withdraw after a triple; realised profit is where composure comes from.
- Decide short-term or mid-term before entering; targets, stops and leverage differ.
- Luck is real, but blow-ups come from oversizing and missing stops.
- If the account declines in steps with deposits and no withdrawals, the method is wrong: stop or rebuild.

The original also covers cash-out channels and borrowing in detail. Those parts carry legal risk and are left out here.
