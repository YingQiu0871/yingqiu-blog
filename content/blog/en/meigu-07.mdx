## Stocks: the price of admission

On State Street's figures SPY compounded at 10.76% a year from its January 1993 launch to October 2026, but holders sat through a drawdown of about 55% in 2007 to 2009. QQQ fell 83% from March 2000 to October 2002 and needed until February 2015 to break even with dividends. IWM beat SPY by a wide margin in 2001 to 2009 and then lagged it badly in 2015 to 2024.

## Products with a catch

ARKK returned 41.3% a year over five years to November 2021, yet Morningstar estimated its investors earned 9.9%, because most of the money arrived after the gains; the fund then fell about 81%. TQQQ resets its triple leverage daily: across 2022 and 2023 QQQ ended up about 4% while TQQQ was still down nearly 40%. USO rolls oil futures, and in 2009 crude rose 78% while the fund gained less than a fifth.

## The supposedly safe ones

TLT holds long Treasuries that cannot default, and it still lost about 48% from August 2020 to October 2023. GLD peaked in August 2011 as the world's largest ETF and then dropped about 45% over four years. XLE lost about two thirds between November 2014 and March 2020, a month before oil futures settled at minus $37.63, and then gained more than 50% and more than 60% in the next two years.

## Key points

- Every asset class here has roughly halved at some point, bonds and gold included.
- Time to recovery hurts more than depth: QQQ took about fifteen years.
- Investor returns trail fund returns when money chases performance.
- Leverage and futures-based funds carry structural decay.
- Know what an ETF actually holds before buying the name on the label.
