## Plot summary

*Forever Young* (无问西东) tells the story of four young people from different eras who all graduated from Tsinghua University: full of hope for their youth, they moved forward through contradictions and struggles amid the changes of their times, and finally found their true selves.

Put more complexly: in the torrent of the times, where should the small individual go? Only — establish virtue, establish words, and ask not about east or west (无问西东).

The film depicts four eras: around the 1920s, the 1930s–40s, the 1960s, and the new millennium's 21st century, spanning a hundred years — from the changes of the Republican era, through the storm of the War of Resistance, to the years of the sixties.

The links between the four stories: the protagonist of the first story becomes the teacher of the second; the protagonist of the second inadvertently saves the protagonist of the third; a supporting character of the third saves the parents of the fourth's protagonist; and the protagonist of the fourth helps a family of quadruplets.

## Review

*Forever Young* mainly tells of four young people from Tsinghua University facing the hardest choices of their different eras: Chen Peng wavers between love and ideals; Zhang Guoguo, after helping the quadruplets, suspects the family of clinging to him, and is schemed out of his job by his superior; Shen Guangyao, ignoring his mother's pleas, throws himself into the war and gives his precious life for his country; Wu Linglan struggles over changing his major…

The whole film explores one question: what is the real? Teacher Mei says: the real is "what you see, what you hear, what you do, and who you are with — a peace and joy welling up from the depths of the heart, without regret or shame." Tagore says: "For you, the world has no meaning and no purpose, yet it is full of whimsical fantasies. But who knows — perhaps in this sultry, weary noon, that stranger passing your door with a basket of marvellous wares, calling out loudly, will startle you from your daze; you will walk out and meet your fate." Yes — in life, we should stop looking around and view everything with a calm heart, seeking our own truth.

What is the real around us? I think we should cherish the present, do well every present thing, rather than worrying unrealistically about the future or aiming too high. And we should stay down to earth, examine ourselves honestly: for us, what is "real"? We should know ourselves truly, know what we need to do now and in the future, and who we want to become. Read more, think more, find the "truth" of what we face or what is happening around us; don't be fooled by appearances — see the truth of things. Treasure the people around us, do the right things at the right time. Most importantly, act from the heart, and keep striving for it, with a clear conscience.

Love what you love, do what you do, listen to your heart, and ask not about east or west.
