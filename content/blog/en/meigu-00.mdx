> Study notes compiled from public sources in November 2026. Figures in each chapter are tied to the sources listed at its end. Markets and rules change. This is not investment advice.

Chinese-language primers on US stocks tend to be either broker tutorials that stop once the order is placed, or lists of tickers without reasons. The English material is far richer but assumes a reader living in the United States. These notes borrow that framework, start from zero, and try to explain why. No stock, fund or broker is recommended; tickers appear only as examples.

## Seven parts

- Chapters 1 to 4, foundations: what this money is for, what a share is, who is in the market, accounts and orders.
- Chapters 5 to 9, horizons and tools: short, medium and long term as three different games, why index funds work, and nine classic ETFs each used to make one point.
- Chapters 10 to 13, fundamentals: the three statements, earnings season, moats and buybacks, valuation.
- Chapters 14 to 16, the wider environment: rates and the Federal Reserve, economic data and currencies, politics and outside shocks.
- Chapters 17 to 20, price itself: trend, technical indicators, sentiment gauges, the market calendar and its plumbing.
- Chapters 21 to 23, machines: quant strategies, factors, and what is real in AI trading.
- Chapters 24 to 28, people: psychology, position sizing, buying and selling, twenty common mistakes, and four bubbles.

## How to read

Reading in order is easiest. Each chapter ends with a short list of what to remember. With little time, start with chapters 1, 5, 6, 7, 24 and 25.

## Limits

These notes come from reading, not from trading. Where sources disagree the text says "about" and names the source; where no primary source was found, the number was left out.
