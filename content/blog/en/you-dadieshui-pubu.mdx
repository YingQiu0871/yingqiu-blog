> Written in Grade 4 at the Affiliated Primary School of Yunnan Normal University, Wuhua District, Kunming · Gu Yuning (originally published on zuowen.com, 2012-07-16)

Shilin is known across the world for its four sights. Early this morning we left the hotel and followed the bumpy mountain road to one of them — the Dadieshui Falls scenic area.

Inside the park, clumps of green bamboo grew thick and lush; tall emerald trees stood everywhere, and now and then a karst cave appeared on the cliff across the valley. Suddenly a low, rumbling sound rose from beneath our feet. I shouted: "An earthquake!" Mum said to me: "It's not an earthquake — it's the roar of the waterfall. It's giving us a welcome speech!" We followed the path down into the valley. On the path, which few people use these days, the steps were already slippery, and landslides had made them even harder to step on.

When we reached the bottom, the view opened up. Fine droplets drifted onto our faces on the wind, like the soft spring rain of February or March. The waterfall's roar was deafening — you could feel its grandeur before you even saw it. A few more steps, and the waterfall's profile appeared before me. Suddenly I spotted a viewing platform on the opposite side and climbed up to it, full of excitement. From the platform, the falls plunged into the deep pool like a white dragon, and that roar might well have been the dragon's joyful cry as it dived in. The hills across the way were a vivid green, making the falls look even fresher. The water of the deep pool was muddy yellow — probably because the torrent had stirred up the loess at the bottom, sending it up to the surface for air.

I walked to the foot of the falls and looked up: the water pouring from the sky made my head spin. Just then Aunt Fu shouted, "Come over here, it's so comfortable!" — a breeze had carried the spray onto us, wonderfully cool. Looking closely, I saw several dark caves under the falls. Wind blew out of them, as if there were giant fans inside, and it gave you the creeps. The water below the falls was "rolled up" by the air currents into balls, light and airy next to the mighty falls, as if floating on the surface.

I noticed water marks beside the falls and thought: three years of drought in a row must have shrunk the flow and the falls themselves. A small river ran along the edge of the deep pool, flowing out of the valley toward the distance, with houses scattered along its banks. This year's steady rain, I imagined, had not only revived the falls but let the people here sow their fields on time.

Don't let this beautiful sight become history. Let us all protect what nature has given us.
