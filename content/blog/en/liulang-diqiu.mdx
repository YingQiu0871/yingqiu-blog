## 1. Summary

Based on Liu Cixin's novella of the same name, the film tells of the sun about to enter its red-giant stage; humanity builds 10,000-plus fusion engines on the Earth's surface to journey to Proxima Centauri, four light-years away. But the road through the universe is full of danger: during the gravitational slingshot around Jupiter, something goes wrong, and the Earth is about to fall into Jupiter. Liu Qi and his sister Duoduo steal their grandfather's licence and skip class to go to the surface; amid the catastrophe they meet a rescue team and rush to save the underground city of Hangzhou. After losing Grandpa and several team members in the Shanghai region, they learn that the Hangzhou underground city has already fallen. They then head for the Sulawesi steering engine.

Meanwhile, on the space station, Liu Peiqiang, with one day left before returning home, is ordered by the AI MOSS to hibernate immediately in "low-energy mode". Suspicious, he looks out the window and finds MOSS has altered its course; judging that MOSS has defected, he smashes the hibernation pod and enters the control room.

On the ground, at the critical moment, people ignite all the steering engines and more than seventy per cent of the planetary engines, but are told they still cannot escape falling into Jupiter. At the final moment, Liu Qi and his team work together: Li Changtiao's "Twelve Strokes of Spring Festival" programme directs the propellant originally sprayed from twelve nozzles into a single nozzle, to reach Jupiter's altitude and ignite the mixture of oxygen from Earth's atmosphere and Jupiter's hydrogen. But as rescue teams from around the world fight desperately, they find the flame's tip still 5,000 kilometres short of Jupiter. At this point Liu Peiqiang destroys MOSS's control, manually pilots the space station, jettisons the hibernation module, accelerates to "Forward Three", and builds the bridge of hope for Earth with the station's 300,000 tonnes of fuel.

Three years later, Liu Qi and Duoduo have joined the engineering corps, pushing the Earth toward Proxima Centauri 2,500 years away.

## 2. Audience comments

> The film is grander and more magnificent than expected. The endless night after the terminator line, the Seed of Fire project, making the Earth wander, Jupiter pushing the Earth… such big designs, big patterns — who would have thought China could shoot science fiction on this scale, and the first one at that? Amazing. Watching foreign sci-fi used to feel so distant; this time, seeing the familiar CCTV towers of Beijing and the Oriental Pearl of Shanghai as frozen post-apocalyptic scenes at minus 89 degrees was both curious and wistful. The plot has its formulas, but for this newborn sci-fi of Chinese cultural background, encouragement outweighs criticism. The director said Americans shoot sci-fi by abandoning Earth and searching for new habitats, while the Chinese refuse to abandon Earth and hold fast to home… "Hope is the only direction home."

> At last, it is our turn to look up at the stars. Post-apocalyptic ruins, cyberpunk underground cities, and vast end-of-the-world imaginings — this is the first true Chinese hard sci-fi. The stunning premise of dragging the Earth out of the solar system is itself a grand projection of "homeland sentiment", exactly as Liu Cixin said: "The sun is dead; the people are still alive." This is by no means merely a genre exercise inside a giant industrial shell; the tragic grandeur that runs through it already has an epic texture — the surprise wrought by Da Liu's imaginative universe and the film team's perfectionism. Qu Chuxiao is very charismatic, bringing to life a brave, cheeky yet tender young hero. Let's hope it becomes a blockbuster, so we can see more Chinese sci-fi films; perhaps better ones will come, but so far this is undoubtedly the best.

> A sad story: the sun is about to die, the Earth is about to wander — and our school uniforms are still this ugly…

> That a Chinese director could make hard sci-fi like this, with this level of completion, is thrilling — it makes you forgive every imperfection! Calling it the first year of Chinese sci-fi cinema would be untenable, since China has long made soft sci-fi; but calling *The Wandering Earth* the first milestone of Chinese hard sci-fi — no one can refute that. Its level of industrialisation is absolutely unprecedented in China; it is a film that deserves a place in Chinese film history! In fact, Chinese audiences are no strangers to this kind of hard sci-fi; it's just that the country never had the industrial foundation for such a large production, perhaps because the Chinese lacked the cultural confidence, couldn't find a uniquely Chinese cultural context, and didn't believe Chinese people could save the Earth and save the world. Until today — finally a director did it, and did it brilliantly, setting a very high industry benchmark for Chinese hard sci-fi. In a Chinese cultural context, saving all of humanity the Chinese way can be just as seamless and just as explosive!

## 3. Review

### (1) Details and Easter eggs

*The Wandering Earth* is full of moving details and Easter eggs — genuinely conscientious filmmaking. Right now I can't get "Beijing District Three Traffic Commission reminds you…" out of my head. Other Easter eggs:

1. The plan to reignite the engines used "saturation rescue". What is saturation rescue? According to the film's data: 76,000 firestones were transported to 5,000 engines by 402,600 people, maximising the chance of reignition. Although the film shows only one small team's deeds, behind them is the struggle of all humanity;
2. The truck driver who crashed killed himself only after saying in Japanese that he wanted a bowl of miso soup; the Russian sacrificed himself to push Liu Peiqiang into the control pod; an American flag lay over a transport truck. At the end, people speaking different languages turned their vehicles around, heading in the direction away from home. This is not a "patriotic film" in the usual sense, but humanity saving itself. The individualistic patriotism of Hollywood blockbusters never appears;
3. In the elevator, a team member died to save Grandpa Han Zi'ang, and Han Zi'ang lived only a few minutes longer. Someone carried the firestone with his life, yet the engine was lit by another team. In many films, deaths are forcibly given meaning — at least they buy something. But here, death is just death: real and cold, like the real world;
4. The five Seed of Fire authorisations MOSS showed Liu Peiqiang came from the US, UK, France, Russia and China — the five permanent members of the UN Security Council;
5. The Seed of Fire space station is like the Blue Space ship drifting alone in the universe. The hibernation pods waiting for rescue after escape recall the Trisolaran fleet that missed Earth after the Deterrence Era. Liu Peiqiang is like the unscrupulous Wade. Add Liu Peiqiang's cry of "Forward Three" when he crashes into Jupiter, and under Da Liu's hand, *The Wandering Earth* has something of *The Three-Body Problem* in it;
6. As an AI, MOSS is probably absolutely rational: its purpose is to ensure the continuation of the human race, and the space station is humanity's last seed. MOSS must have calculated the plan of crashing the station into Jupiter too — but because that would sacrifice the station, as long as there was a 1% chance of failure, it would not choose that. And after Liu Peiqiang burned the station's stored data, it was impossible to continue human civilisation with the station, so in the end MOSS and the command centre had to accept the collision plan;
7. After the Earth stopped rotating, one half was perpetual day, the other perpetual night. The day half faces the sun and is covered with planetary engines to escape the sun's gravity; the night half is the direction of the Earth's travel. That is why Liu Qi cannot see his father's star — because for all these years, Beijing has been in daylight;
8. Grandpa Han Zi'ang was born in 1999 — a genuine 90s kid — which is why he uses an old phone, scrolls Douyin, and listens to "Seaweed Dance";
9. Personnel transport vehicles hang wind chimes; Han Zi'ang's truck hangs Buddhist beads and a Maitreya statue; the Russian longs for his vodka. Far from home, this is how they go home;
10. Where no plants grow, the earthworm, which can live on soil alone, may be the best source of protein. "Durian-flavoured mealworm jerky, the perfect afternoon snack, a great gift for friends and family." But it probably doesn't taste good — one dying team member said he wanted a bowl of rice; he hadn't had one in at least 20 years;
11. As the second largest body in the solar system, Jupiter's composition is close to the sun's: about 88% hydrogen and 12% helium — like the leftover scraps of the sun's formation. Without an oxidiser, hydrogen cannot ignite by itself. But when Earth's atmosphere and Jupiter's atmosphere attract and mingle, Jupiter's hydrogen and Earth's oxygen can reach a 2:1 hydrogen-oxygen detonation ratio; a single spark can set off a violent explosion that pushes the Earth away. (Considering whether the oxygen is sufficient, the directionality of the explosion, and so on, this might not work in reality — but in a sci-fi film, it's self-consistent enough);
12. When the elevator reached the surface and the door opened, everyone bent down against the wind and snow rushing in — only Liu Qi and Han Duoduo stood there foolishly, showing this really was their first time on the frozen ground;
13. In the dust-filled wiring room, the engineer took out a box holding cigarettes and a note. The paper inside, reading "remember to wear your long johns", had long yellowed. The middle-aged engineer was once a child whose mother nagged him to wear long johns. But because of the lottery, or a tsunami, his mother had been gone for a long time;
14. Many films cannot escape the shackles of the great-man view of history, making people believe that saving the world and changing history are the work of a few individuals. In *The Wandering Earth*, when Liu Peiqiang flies over the station, he finds others blasting their way into compartments, resisting the Seed of Fire project; when he thinks of blowing up Jupiter to push the Earth, the coalition government tells him Israeli scientists already thought of it; as the Sulawesi engine tries to ignite Jupiter, two other engines also raise flames 5,000 kilometres high. History is never the struggle of individuals, but a blurry necessity plus a bit of luck;
15. "Gangzi is gone!" is a tribute to the firefighters of the Tianjin explosion;
16. The order in which the international rescue teams later arrived at the steering engine matched the order of foreign rescue teams arriving after the 2008 Wenchuan earthquake.

### (2) Very Chinese

Whether the charming heavy-industrial steampunk or the ideal communist society, both are entrancing. The lion dances, skewers, mahjong tables and firecrackers of the underground city; the slogans on the surface — "Beijing District Three…", "Production safety…", "Safety first…" — are also a feature. The buildings in the film are grand and magnificent, hard and bold, with a cruel metallic breath; a person before them is infinitely small — the aura of a nation of infrastructure maniacs hits you in the face.

### (3) A Chinese view of society

In similar American disaster films, the same story repeats: the world is about to end, the idiotic government does nothing to help the hero and obstructs him at every turn, and the hero — black girlfriend in one hand, machine gun in the other, maybe with a black brother, an Asian programmer and a lesbian couple — saves the world. Even in interstellar films, Americans are steadfast ship-faction members, always flying away on a single ship. But China is different. The softest part of a Chinese heart is reserved for "home". If it's only yourself, is that a family? And so: "If we go, we go together." Moreover, the Chinese have never lacked the spirit of "man conquers nature": Yu Gong moving mountains, Jing Wei filling the sea, Yu the Great taming the floods — fighting fate itself, defying heaven to live. We believe only in saving ourselves, and we are willing to act: one heart, a million hands, giving everything, striving with all our might.

### (4) Characters who move you because they are real

No character in the film is a superhero; they are all ordinary people with some shining quality. When the Hangzhou underground city fell, the one who first lit the Sulawesi engine was not the protagonist; even the idea of igniting Jupiter came first not from the protagonist but from Israeli scientists.

### (5) Ideology

Overall coordination, abolishing money, focusing on heavy industry, concentrating strength to do great things, working together for a grand goal — all the signs point to a distinct political and economic system, recalling the magnificent age when the red tide swept the world.

This is also very reasonable. Facing great disasters, humanity tends to spontaneously form communal organisations. Even in America, from the 1906 San Francisco earthquake to the hurricanes of recent years, victims quickly organised mutual-aid communities to save themselves. When resources are scarce and survival is at stake, centralisation is indeed the only way out.

Huang Renyu noted in *China: A Macro History* that China was able to form unified dynasties so early precisely because the Yellow River flooded constantly, requiring a unified central power to coordinate resources and control the floods.

The coalition government uses French rather than English — perhaps related to France's communist tradition. But a more reasonable explanation is the self-destruction of English-speaking countries. Throughout the film, almost no American or British power appears; from today's perspective, that is somewhat unusual.

We can also speculate that in the face of such an apocalypse, democratic countries are prone to dragging their feet. Perhaps America would elect a president who, going his own way, withdraws from the "Paris Accord", insists the sun's expansion and global warming are "fake news", puts American interests first, and cares nothing about whether civilisation continues a hundred generations later.

Human individuals are short-sighted. Getting people to give up present pleasures and worry about things two thousand years from now — mobilising that through democratic institutions — I remain sceptical. Never mind building an engine with the strength of a whole nation: look, the beacon of democracy can't even agree on building a wall without the government shutting down. A value system that sacrifices half of humanity for a grand enterprise could never be accepted.
