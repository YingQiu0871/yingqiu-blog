## Three things under one label

AIEQ, an ETF launched in October 2017 whose model screens more than 6,000 US companies a day, returned 6.7% a year from November 2017 to June 2023 against 11.2% for a total-market index fund. The Eurekahedge AI hedge fund index made about 10% a year from December 2009 to July 2024, against about 14% for the S&P 500. The best users of machine learning do not report results at all. Much of the edge lies in data others lack: a Berkeley study of satellite images of retail car parks found 4% to 5% around earnings dates, with retail investors on the losing side.

## What the language-model papers show

Lopez-Lira and Tang had ChatGPT score 134,129 headlines; the long-short portfolio gained over 650% before costs, but nothing at 0.2% per round trip, and its Sharpe ratio fell each year. Kim, Muhn and Nikolaev reported about 60% accuracy on earnings direction; a later study with another setup found 47.7%.

## Look-ahead bias

A model trained through 2023 already knows how 2020 turned out, so testing it on 2020 news is like re-sitting an exam after reading the answers. Glasserman and Lin measured this by anonymising company names.

## Key points

- Fund machine learning, language models and retail chatbots are separate topics.
- Public AI funds have not beaten the index.
- Paper profits sit in small caps and shorts and shrink after costs.
- A backtest inside a model's training window proves little.
- Owning AI stocks such as Nvidia is not the same as trading with AI.
