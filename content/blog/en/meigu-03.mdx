## Exchanges matter less than indexes

The NYSE traces back to the 1792 Buttonwood Agreement; Nasdaq opened in 1971 as the first fully electronic market. For an individual buyer the listing venue changes almost nothing. The three headline indexes are another matter, because each is built differently.

## Three rulers

The Dow holds 30 stocks weighted by share price. On November 20, 2026 Goldman Sachs at about $791 carried roughly 9.8% of it while Nvidia at about $126 carried 1.6%, although Nvidia is the second-largest company in the S&P 500. When Apple split four-for-one in 2020, the index provider replaced three members to restore the technology weight. The S&P 500 weights by float-adjusted market value, requires positive recent earnings and leaves the final call to a committee. The Nasdaq-100 takes the 100 largest non-financial Nasdaq listings and is about two-thirds technology.

## Turnover and concentration

General Electric, the last of the original 1896 Dow stocks, was removed in June 2018 after falling 57% in two years; its replacement, Walgreens, was itself swapped for Amazon in 2024. Index returns owe something to this pruning, which single-stock holders never receive. Meanwhile seven companies made up about 33.7% of the S&P 500 in November 2026.

## Key points

- Know how an index is weighted before reading its moves.
- A broad index fund is less evenly spread than "500 companies" suggests.
- Most trading is institutional; only about 19% of US families hold individual stocks directly.
- Unlike China's A-shares, US stocks have no daily price limits, only market-wide circuit breakers at 7%, 13% and 20%.
