## Fixed dates

Some volume has nothing to do with opinion. On the third Friday of March, June, September and December, index futures, index options and stock options expire together, and positions are closed or rolled. Index funds must buy new members at the close before a change takes effect. Tesla joined the S&P 500 on 21 December 2020 as its sixth-largest member. Yet the index effect has faded: Greenwood and Sammon measure the average abnormal gain for additions at 7.6% in the 1990s and 0.8% in the past decade.

## Where orders go

Zero-commission brokers send retail orders to wholesalers, who pay for them because small, uncorrelated orders are safe to trade against. US brokers received about $2.5 billion in such payments in 2020. The SEC staff calls the arrangement a conflict of interest for the broker.

## Options pushing stocks

A dealer who sells calls hedges by buying shares, and must buy more as the price rises toward the strike. That feedback is a gamma squeeze, and it is strongest near expiry. Same-day options were about 43% of S&P 500 index option volume in 2023, according to Cboe. GameStop in January 2021 is usually told as a gamma squeeze, but the SEC staff report found no evidence of one; sustained retail buying carried the price.

## Key points

- Expiration and rebalancing days inflate volume mechanically.
- Front-running index additions no longer pays much.
- Free trading is funded by payment for order flow.
- Option hedging can amplify moves, most of all close to expiry.
- Even regulators with full data found the popular GameStop story incomplete.
