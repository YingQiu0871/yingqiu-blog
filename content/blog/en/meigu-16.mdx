## Elections

Vanguard's data from 1860 to 2020 show a 60/40 portfolio returning 8.7% in presidential election years and 7.7% in other years, a gap that is not statistically significant, and almost identical results under either party. Fidelity finds midterm years bumpy, with drawdowns near 18%, while the S&P 500 has not fallen in the twelve months after a midterm since 1942. That is 21 cases, a small sample.

## Tariffs, shutdowns and downgrades

Tariffs hit costs directly, so reactions are large. In April 2025 the S&P 500 lost 10.5% in two days, gained 9.5% on the day a 90-day pause was announced, and set a record by June 27. Shutdowns barely register: the index rose during both the 35-day one in 2018-19 and the 43-day one in 2025, although official data went missing. After the 2011 downgrade stocks fell 6.7% in a day while investors bought Treasuries.

## Wars and the pandemic

LPL's table of geopolitical events shows the S&P 500 bottoming in about 18 days and recovering in under 39 on average. COVID was different because it removed revenue directly: a 34% fall in 33 days.

## Key points

- Ask whether an event changes earnings or interest rates for years.
- Most headline shocks fade within weeks for US stocks.
- Policy-driven falls can be reversed by the next announcement, which makes them hard to trade.
- The slower channel is inflation and rates, as in 2022.
