## A list of mistakes by stage

The Chinese chapter lists twenty mistakes in the order a new investor meets them. Before entering: using money needed soon, not choosing a time horizon, buying products not understood (TQQQ lost about 79% in 2022), and mistaking early luck for skill. When picking stocks: buying a familiar name without checking, as when buyers piled into the wrong Zoom ticker in March 2020; confusing a good company with a good price; chasing last year's winner; and calling seven technology stocks a diversified portfolio. While holding: watching the account daily, selling winners and keeping losers, averaging down without rechecking the reason. After losses and gains: doubling up, selling out at the bottom for good, switching methods too often, keeping no records, adding leverage.

## Noise and fraud

Reported investment-scam losses in the United States reached 7.9 billion dollars in 2025, according to FTC data. The oldest pattern is the pump and dump. In December 2022 the SEC charged eight social-media personalities over an alleged 100 million dollar scheme run through Twitter and Discord; these remain allegations, and the criminal trial of seven defendants is scheduled for May 2027. FINRA warns of a newer variant in which fake advisers advertise on social platforms and steer victims into chat groups.

## Key points

- Most mistakes are made before the order is placed.
- Written rules guard against holding losers and adding to them.
- Doubling up after losses and leveraging after gains come from the same impulse.
- Check registration on BrokerCheck or Investor.gov, and ignore anyone promising guaranteed returns.
