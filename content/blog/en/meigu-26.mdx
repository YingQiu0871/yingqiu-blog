## Buying

There are three common ways to put a sum to work. Investing it all at once came out ahead of spreading it over three months 68% of the time in Vanguard's study of 1976 to 2022. Buying in instalments gives up a little expected return in exchange for less regret. Waiting for a dip is the popular choice and the weakest, because it requires being right twice.

## Selling

Three reasons hold up: the original case for the purchase has broken, the position has outgrown its limit, or the money is needed elsewhere. Price alone is not a reason. SoftBank sold its Nvidia stake in January 2019 for about 3.6 billion dollars; Enron staff held their own company's shares from above 90 dollars to under one. Bessembinder's data explains both stories: 58% of US stocks have trailed Treasury bills over their lifetimes, and about 4% produced all the excess wealth.

## Stops and staying put

Stop-losses are essential for short-term traders and mostly a burden for long-term index holders. Research by Kaminski and Lo finds they help only when losses tend to continue. The best and worst days cluster, so people who leave after a crash usually miss the rebound.

## Key points

- Lump-sum buying wins more often; instalments buy peace of mind.
- Sell on a broken thesis, an oversized position, or a need for cash.
- Single stocks need their thesis checked; an index can simply be held.
- Missing the ten best days in twenty years cut the annual return from 6.06% to 2.44%.
