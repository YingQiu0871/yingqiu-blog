## One logic for most entries

Almost every long this week used the same setup: room above, a reasonably long consolidation, and an entry at the turning point where price starts to lift. What failed were breakouts taken after the move had started: LINK and TRB breakouts at the top of a high consolidation all round-tripped. The launch-point entry holds; the breakout at the high comes straight back. Shorts mirror this. On MEME and GAS I shorted the breakdown inside a correction instead of the level above it.

## Right structure, wrong price

My favorite short is a completed five-wave rise followed by a false breakout that reverses, the same as the PERP trade. TRB printed it and I expected at least 75. The correct entry is the bar that engulfs the false break. I missed it, chased a short that bounced hard, then chased again at 123 and could not hold to the target. However strong the thesis, a bad entry price makes the trade unmanageable.

## The BNT night

During a market-wide crash I lost several thousand on two counter-trend bounces, then went all-in on BNT as the strongest coin when BTC stabilized. It was a heavy position on a high-level breakout in a thin market: the eight-point red bar was my own exit. Adding cost me the core position I had held toward 1, about 400,000 in missed profit, 500,000 for the night. Be careful adding at highs whatever the logic, and never size heavily in an illiquid coin.

## Three-month roadmap

BTC should run to about 45,000, top this leg above 40,000, correct for 20 to 30 days, then make a second leg to 55,000 to 58,000. ETH goes to 3,000 first and breaks out ahead of BTC. The cycle runs about two and a half months, into year end. The one coin to watch is ORDI, the Dogecoin of this bull market: enter only after its consolidation ends.

## Key points

- Enter at the turning point after a long consolidation, never at a breakout from a high range.
- Trend shorts go at the level above, not on the breakdown bar.
- A correct thesis with a wrong entry price still loses.
- Hold the first small divergence of a new leg; skip the second and third.
- Adding at highs in an illiquid coin can destroy a good core position.
- A turning point is where price stops falling; enter there with a stop, sized so three misses are survivable.
