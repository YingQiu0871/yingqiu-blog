## The arithmetic of drawdowns

A 20% loss needs a 25% gain to recover; a 50% loss needs 100%. TQQQ, the triple-leveraged Nasdaq-100 fund, returned roughly minus 79% in 2022 while its index fell about 33%, so its holders needed the fund to multiply almost five times. Daily leverage resets make this worse in choppy markets, which is why the SEC and FINRA issued a joint alert on these products.

## Limits, cash and false diversification

Sizing answers two questions: how much in any one stock, and how much in stocks overall. A 5% cap means a bankruptcy costs 5% of the account. Owning many names is no protection if they fall together. In 2022 the seven largest technology companies lost between 26% and 65% each. Correlations also rise in a panic, exactly when diversification is needed.

## What borrowing and shorting cost

Margin takes away the ability to wait: a broker can sell your shares at the low without calling first. Archegos, running concentrated positions through swaps at several banks, collapsed within days in March 2021 and cost its lenders about ten billion dollars. Short sellers pay borrow fees and dividends and face losses with no ceiling.

## Key points

- Deeper losses need disproportionately larger gains to recover.
- Work out the worst case for a position before placing the order.
- Diversification is about what falls together, and that changes under stress.
- In a favourable coin-flip experiment 28% of players still went bust; betting twice the Kelly fraction erases the edge.
