> Class 1, Grade 11 · Gu Yuning · Teacher: Wu Yanling

The Spring Festival of 2020 was destined to be the most unforgettable Chinese New Year in recent years. The truth about the pneumonia and the novel coronavirus, city lockdowns — a flood of information crashed into our lives, washing away the flavour of the holiday and scattering families that should have gathered. In this "war against the epidemic" that touched everyone's heart, many things deserve our reflection. "With another person as your mirror, you can see gain and loss." The epidemic was like a mirror, reflecting the habits of thought hidden in people's minds.

Why did society fall into panic, everyone in chaos, frantic as ants on a hot pan? The main reason is the herd mentality that never leaves people's minds.

From a few days before the New Year, rumours spread online. By the time cities began locking down after the holiday, the messages online were a carnival of demons: helpless masses like a small boat in a dark procession of ghosts, watching demons and monsters spew words of dubious truth and great shock. Everyone in it was an accomplice to the ill intentions of the scheming. "In an avalanche, no snowflake is innocent" — this was precisely the "achievement" of the muddled masses drifting on a sea of uncertainty. Take a relative of mine: from a few days before New Year, she grew wildly excited, searching the internet with all her might for gossip, pushing her conspiracy-laden theories on everyone, unstoppable — even during the Spring Festival Gala she kept "reporting" one "the truth is like this!" after another and "Shocking! Don't share and you're not Chinese!", disturbing the whole family. On the morning of New Year's Day she dragged me out to buy out every pharmacy in Wuhua District, spending thousands of yuan on medicine completely unrelated to fighting the virus, enough for half a lifetime. A few days later came the rumour that Shuanghuanglian was miraculous against pneumonia. It was just another groundless "great secret", no different from "an ancestral Chinese doctor cures COVID" or "one trick to never catch COVID". Only this time the rumour-mongers had upped their game, borrowing the gold-letter signboard of the Shanghai Institute of Materia Medica, while quietly swapping the real subject of the research for Shuanghuanglian oral liquid. Half-true, half-false, mixing real and fake — truly able to fool people. The whole country went mad: online pharmacies were cleaned out, offline ones even more so, and some people even came to blows over an extra box of oral liquid. Most bizarre of all, even the "double-huang" lotus-seed-paste mooncakes on Taobao were snapped up by careless, moneyed buyers. This was just a microcosm of the social phenomenon. Everyone seemed to have lost reason; the whole nation fell into a suffocating, doomsday-survival atmosphere.

This makes the value of independent thinking all the clearer. In the epidemic, fear is understandable, because we know we face danger beyond our prediction and control: worrying whether we or our loved ones will be infected, whether infection will threaten life. But precisely for this reason, we should escape the nameless agitation in our hearts and step out of the swamp of anxiety. Sit down, think more, objectively consider what stage the epidemic has reached and the reasoning behind the government's corresponding measures. Still less should we listen to those seemingly reasonable, shocking, sensational rumours online. The gossip is not to be trusted. To learn about the novel coronavirus, we should seek rigorous, matter-of-fact papers, gather information from different channels, cross-check it, and reach scientific, objective conclusions about the virus's nature, its pathology, and scientific personal protection. But independent thinking does not mean blind optimism. Most rumours wildly exaggerate the epidemic's horror and the collapse of the situation; some, however, simplify it. Things are neither that serious nor that optimistic. "Humans are born small, yet great because they think." What distinguishes humans from other animals is reason — the ability to use what we have learned to see through the layers of fog and grasp the essence of things.

What we truly should do, from the perspective of the family and the country, is find ways to solve the problem and prevent similar events: what laws and regulations should be enacted, how to revise the Food Safety Law according to actual conditions, offer suggestions to the state, and make meaningful contributions to society. From the individual's perspective, there is no point arguing further when things have reached such a pass — all empty talk. Doing personal hygiene well is already a great contribution. To use a popular phrase, "live in the present": when the situation is like this, stay home and enjoy the rare chance to be with family, the beauty of food, or the bizarre futures of science-fiction films. This, too, is a contribution to society: if we cannot work on the front line, at least we don't add to the country's troubles.

Therefore the most important task now is to cool everyone down, to make people stop their headlong rush and ask themselves: "Am I doing the right thing? Is my thinking objective?" Happily, thanks to the government's correct guidance and the promotion of positive energy, people have gradually recovered their reason and returned to the right track. And thanks to the self-sacrifice of countless "counter-marching" heroes, and the help of people around the world who care for and support China, the epidemic has been turning for the better. However long the night, the day will come.

Perhaps many people like to use their weaknesses as a shield: "I'm just stupid, just incapable, I can't see through it." As the saying goes, "running away is shameful but useful." But what drives history forward is the spirit of doing what seems impossible. Refusing to change, resting content with the status quo, can never bring us good. Only the spirit of breakthrough and enterprise can help us in the life ahead. Finding a problem is for solving it. So let us dare to face the defects in our characters, pull ourselves out of the swamp of conformity, first achieve independence of spirit, and then strive to shape our ability to think independently and view problems objectively — to create free thought!
